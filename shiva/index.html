Shiva

📆 Created on July 31, 2026.

Shiva passed away in 2017 and I previously wrote about the passing of (Manny)[/manny] which happened in 2021 and even though it has been longer since she went, this post has been a lot harder for me to sit down and write. I guess that's because Shiva was the first loss of someone close to me. I mean I've lost uncles, aunts, cousins, grand-parents, and friends but I spent 12 years with Shiva in my life every day. Losing Shiva and Manny each had a... sharpness that I hadn't felt before. Thinking about this now, it's not just that she was the first, although that definitely factors in, no the main source of the grief, guilt and pain is two fold:

  • She was my responsibility
  • I was there when she passed
  • I (partly) made the decision for her passing to happen

Losing someone you've cared for is different, for me at least. When you're with them it never occurs you that you should do anything different, not really, not in the ways it will when they're gone. In the days after we said goodbye to Shiva I spent a lot of time wondering what could or should have been done differently, not just things I could have done to help make her final moments or days better, but what I could have done in those times when she wasn't sick to have more memories of her.

Did that work trip really need to happen? Could I have missed this event or gathering? It loops constantly in the days after her passing, and even now eight years on I can easily find myself in a spiral of circular thinking that crushes me.

Her loss, like Mannys, is still incredibly raw. It's not something I can talk about and keep composure even all these years later. I don't think it ever truly will be, and I don't really want it to be. I'm ok with being devastated if it lets me remember them - the hard part is not getting lost in the guilt or being sucked back into those moments at the veterinarians office. Trying to stay in the positive moments and the good times, that's the best thing I can do for them.

I first met Shiva in 2005, she was a stray that visited a girl I was seeing at the time, we'll call her Amy, out in Fitchburg, Massachusetts. Amy had felt sorry for Shiva, and had started giving her some food and the occaisional pat. Sometimes Shiva would bring a trophy for us to admire at the doorstep. This went on for a few weeks and Shiva's visits got more frequent, as did the trophies. The weather was becoming cooler as the seasons were tansitioning from fall into winter. It was obvious that Shiva was not a house cat out on the prowl at night and she wasn't likely to survive on her own in the winter so Amy decided to take her in.

Amy eventually had to move away, she was changing jobs and wasn't able to find a place that would take cats, she also wasn't sure Shiva would be happy with the move, so I offered to take her in. I had been wanting a cat for a while, I was only a few months away from getting Manny as it would turn out. So Shiva joined me at my apartment in 2005 and she was one of the best things that ever happened to me.

Shiva loved people. She was never nervous around anyone, figuring everyone would give her some sort of attention or treats. She had a "thing" she would do when rubbing up against my wife and I, where she would sort of charge into you a bit, bumping you with her head and then she would rub her face on you wherever you'd let her.

Shiva was incredible affectionate, she enjoyed walking up to a new person, rubbing on them and enjoying the pats that would follow. She was so soft too. I'm not sure why her fur was as soft as it was, but it gave you an easy excuse to keep patting her.

Shiva was also incredibly curious. If my wife and I weren't in the same room as her she would either be with my wife or patroling the house, making sure things were as they should be.

Shiva loved watching birds, we had so many cat towers placed in front of windows that overlooked bird feeders for her. Later on, when we would be at the veterinarian's office we discovered that she loved watching birds on youtube as well. The videos helped distract her from the fact that she was somewhere she didn't want to be - at least it seemed to.

I hope this has shown what a truly special and extraordinary part of our lives Shiva was and how much we still love and miss her. Thank you for visiting and reading, if you'd like to, you can contact me on social media and share some memories of your own furry friends.
